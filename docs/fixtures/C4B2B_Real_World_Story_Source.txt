Blue Signal — Diagnostic Console Inspection

An unoccupied engineering inspection bay aboard a deep-space survey vessel is preparing for an automated systems check. The room is practical and quiet, with neutral grey panels, diffuse overhead utility lights and no crew present. A compact maintenance workstation stands to one side. Beside it, clearly separate and not physically joined to it, is a freestanding diagnostic console.

The diagnostic console has an upright, rectangular housing made of matte graphite-coated metal. Its front face contains one broad cobalt-blue display. Directly below the display is a shallow horizontal control shelf carrying exactly three small circular buttons in a straight row. To the right of the buttons sits one rotary control dial inside a circular recess. The outermost surface of the dial must remain below the surrounding shelf plane, so the dial appears unmistakably recessed, not raised above the shelf. Vertical ribbed grip panels extend along both sides of the console's housing. No lettering, brands, logos, extra screens or additional controls are visible.

An automated inspection camera advances slowly from a three-quarter front-left view towards the console. The camera holds the console and the nearby separate maintenance workstation in a natural industrial composition, allowing both objects to remain distinct. Soft reflections skim the metal housing; the blue display casts a restrained glow. The buttons and recessed dial become clear in the closer view. No objects move and no operator enters the room.

A single brief status tone signals that the diagnostics have finished. The camera settles and holds its view of the console for a moment. The sequence ends without a cut to a different room or a new piece of equipment.

This standalone, original test Story is intended for one short, approximately 18-second cinematic sequence. The governed production plan must be reviewed before any duration or shot count is accepted.
